I co-founded a software company at 22

Before I wrote software for shops, I worked in one. I know what a counter looks like at closing time, when the cash, the stock and the notebook do not agree.

From 2020 to 2022 I was the cashier and junior technician at Cornerstore in Molo. I served customers at the till, then repaired the computers, printers and network that kept the shop running. In 2022 I joined Bizwiz Limited as a junior software engineer and began a degree in Applied Computer Science at Chuka University, so I learned to build software properly while still remembering who it was for.

What I kept seeing was the same in every small business I met: they ran on notebooks, WhatsApp messages and memory. Stock went missing and nobody could say when. Rent was paid and never recorded. Fuel disappeared between the tank and the pump. Drivers finished trips with no paper trail. The owners were working hard, yet they could not answer the simplest question about their own business: where did the money go?

The technology to answer that question already existed. It just was not made for a shop in Kenya that takes M-Pesa at the counter, files tax through eTIMS, and cannot pay enterprise prices. So in July 2024 I co-founded Flexbyte Solutions and started building it.

Stepstar Logistics, a haulage company, was tracking trips in Excel and writing every invoice by hand. Today it runs on a platform I built, covering trip logging, driver mileage pay, fuel records and invoicing. Stephen, who runs it, put it better than I can: “I now track every shilling in my business.”

That sentence is the reason Flexbyte exists. Since then the work has grown into a family of systems for shops, landlords, fuel stations and transport companies, each one built end to end by a small team that still answers its own phone.

What I do at AfCEN

Since August 2026 I have also worked with Africa Climate and Energy Nexus (AfCEN) as a consultant software engineer. I am building the WAIIS Deal Room, the platform where African enterprises meet investors at the WAIIS 2026 summit, and I work on the AIS and have supported the development of other AfCEN products.

The Deal Room exists because talent is not the gap in Africa. Visibility is. A good business that investors cannot see does not get funded, and that is the same problem I started Flexbyte to solve, only at a different scale: a shop that cannot see its own numbers cannot grow either.

The two roles sharpen each other. One day I am building a platform that puts prepared projects in front of investors, and the next I am beside a cashier, watching where a till screen slows them down.

Where this is going

Millions of small businesses across Africa are still one lost notebook away from not knowing what they are owed. Each system I build is designed to serve many businesses from one platform, so what works for one haulage company or one landlord can be offered to the next hundred.

I am looking for investors who want to back software built from inside this market, partners who can bring these systems to the businesses that need them, and business owners ready to be among the first to use them.

I also take on consulting work. If your organisation needs a platform designed and built, or an engineer who can turn a business problem into working software, I am available for consulting and contract engagements.
